## 1. FULL UGC EXAMPLE PROMPTS (copy-paste, then swap subject/product)

### A. Honest Review (no-makeup morning review)
```
A 26-year-old South Asian woman sits casually on her bathroom counter in the morning, no makeup, just woken up. She holds a small amber glass serum bottle up to the camera, squinting slightly like she is genuinely thinking.
0 to 3s: She says directly to camera, "Okay I need to talk about this." Handheld camera, natural bathroom lighting, slightly overexposed.
4 to 9s: She presses two fingers to her cheek and shows the bottle label close up.
10 to 15s: She looks back at camera, grins, says "You will thank me later" and the camera slowly pulls back.
Warm tone, documentary feel, 9:16 vertical, ambient morning sound.
```

### B. Founder Style (brand owner tells the story)
```
A 35-year-old woman at a clean kitchen table, minimal branding visible in background. She looks directly into camera with a calm, knowledgeable expression and no script energy.
0 to 4s: "We built this because I could not find anything that actually worked for me." She sets a product bottle on the table. Soft overhead lighting, fixed stable camera.
5 to 10s: She slides it closer to camera and explains one thing about it.
11 to 15s: She looks up from the product to camera and says "That is why I made this." Long beat. Holds eye contact.
Warm, premium feel, 9:16 vertical.
```

### C. Street Interview (person stopped on the sidewalk)
```
Outdoor street setting, busy sidewalk slightly blurred in background. A 22-year-old man stops mid-walk, turns to camera. Hoodie, earbuds around his neck.
0 to 3s: "Someone just told me about this and I had to try it." He pulls the product out of his jacket pocket. Handheld shaky cam, natural city sounds.
4 to 9s: He holds it up, turns it over once. "Okay it is actually exactly what they said." Close-up on his face.
10 to 15s: He looks back at camera, laughing slightly, says "Yeah I am sold" and walks off frame.
Authentic vibe, 9:16, no music, street ambient audio.
```

### D. Product UGC skeleton (Diet Coke pattern — build your own from this)
Identity block (same face, pores, freckles, realistic hands) + product block (preserve exact branding, logo, label text e.g. "12 Cans", proportions, no warp) + 4 timestamped beats with dialogue + close with: `No music. No voiceover. No on-screen text. No cinematic lighting.`
UGC realism keywords to sprinkle: `Ambient room tone, no music` · `Exactly two arms, five fingers per hand` · `Single-take recording, no cuts` · `slightly imperfect skin texture` · `slight overexposure`.

Reusable openers:
- Talking-head hook: `Mid-twenties woman, shoulder-length brown hair, no makeup, cream crewneck, softly lit kitchen at golden hour. Phone front camera selfie framing, slight handheld micro-movement. She looks slightly off-camera then at the lens: "Okay I have to tell you about this — I genuinely cannot believe how well this worked."`
- Product-in-hand (TikTok interrupted cadence): `"Wait — wait. I have to actually show you this."` + sitting on edge of bed, morning light, arm's-length selfie, raised eyebrows.
- Unboxing (one genuine micro-expression): `sitting at sunlit white desk, small kraft-paper box, slides a finger under the seal, pulls it open, lifts out [PRODUCT], turns it slowly, laughs once` + `"Okay this is way nicer than I thought it would be."`

## 2. CINEMATIC / TIMELINE / MULTI-SHOT EXAMPLES

### Two-character neo-noir anchor (single 8s shot — the gold-standard blocking prompt)
```
Duration: 8 seconds. Aspect ratio: 16:9. One continuous shot.
Spatial blocking: Character A, the woman from Image 1, anchored in the left third, x=30%, foreground, medium shot waist up. Character B, the masked man, right third, x=72%, midground, slightly farther from camera. Center stays open as tense negative space. A faces screen-right toward B; B faces screen-left toward A. They never swap sides, never cross the center line, never change depth layers.
Character A anchor: keeps the same face, hairstyle, black coat, proportions, silhouette. Both feet planted on wet asphalt, shoulders tense, right hand gripping the doorframe, eyes locked on B. Only breathing, hair strands, and coat fabric move.
Character B anchor: upright and still in the right third, holding a silver briefcase at thigh level. Mask, posture, briefcase, and position stable throughout.
Action: A slowly tightens her grip and takes one shallow breath. B does not approach; he only tilts his head slightly.
Camera: medium 35mm anamorphic, eye-level, slow controlled dolly-in from a fixed frontal angle preserving the left/right blocking. Shallow depth of field, rack focus from A's hand to B's mask.
Environment: rain-soaked alley at night, neon reflections on wet asphalt, steam from a vent.
Aesthetics: neo-noir, practical neon, blue-magenta grade, realistic rain, subtle film grain, soft halation.
Audio: rain on metal, distant city hum, briefcase latch click, no music.
Final frame: holds on the negative space between them, A's hand sharp in the foreground, B's mask soft in the right midground.
```

### Timeline prompt skeleton (multi-beat single clip)
Open with type + length + quality, then timecode each beat, one action + (optional) one camera word each:
```
15-second cinematic sequence, ultra-realistic.
[0:00-0:03] Subject + first action.
[0:03-0:07] second action.
[0:07-0:11] third action.
[0:11-0:15] closing action + final frame.
Total: 15s / 16:9.
```
Golden rule: state order + action each beat; add a camera word (CUT to / push-in / hold) only on beats where meaning needs it.

### Multi-shot from storyboard frames (i2v, 2 frames → 2 shots)
```
Cinematic two-shot golden hour desert sequence, anamorphic film look, consistent character and lighting throughout.
Shot 1 (4s) [Image 1]: wide establishing — the nomad walks across the orange dune toward the distant oasis, robe trailing in the wind, slow steady camera.
Shot 2 (6s) [Image 2]: medium close-up at the oasis — the nomad kneels and slowly cups water, lifting it toward his face, droplets fall back into the pool, dust particles drift in the light, gentle slow push-in.
Match-cut transition between shots.
```

## 3. REUSABLE STYLE STACKS (paste into the Style slot)

### Realistic Cinematic (ARRI look — fashion / MV / premium)
> "Ultra-realistic cinematic look, ARRI Alexa LF large-format texture, 50mm lens, shallow depth of field, soft volumetric window light, muted warm-green palette, high dynamic range, natural skin detail, realistic fabric fibers, hair strands catching rim light, subtle film grain."
- CONTROLS for real: ARRI Alexa LF texture, soft volumetric window light, muted warm-green palette, natural skin detail, realistic fabric fibers, hair rim light, film grain (these fight AI plastic skin).
- Just VIBE (not obeyed exactly): per-shot aperture swaps, listing several lenses at once — the model reads it as generic "shallow cinematic DOF."
- Cut as redundant: doubled "shallow DOF", "no cartoon" next to "ultra-realistic", mixing "IMAX + Alexa LF" (pick one).
- Direction = maximally slick → the OPPOSITE of the iPhone look; risks a dreamy AI-tell if motion is lifeless (grain + skin/fabric detail buys it back).

### Anti-AI / Authentic (fight the AI tell)
> "Shot on iPhone 14 Pro, imperfections present, natural available light, handheld, environment sound only."
- Principle: lock non-negotiables (identity/costume) with a reference image → let the shot go freestyle → the imperfect look makes it "alive."

### NatGeo / Documentary
> "National Geographic Wildlife Documentary, IMAX film quality, slow-motion" — locks a nature-doc look; pair with slow-motion for a hunt/reveal beat.

## 5. CAMERA LIBRARY

### 8 core movements (one per shot)
push-in (emotion) · pull-out (reveal context) · pan (horizontal track) · tracking (follow action) · orbit (product/portrait) · aerial (scale/landscape) · handheld (documentary) · fixed (emphasize subject action).

### 14 cinematic techniques (name + when)
Follow+Orbit (character reveal) · Rise+Tilt+Pan (reveal big scale) · God's Eye/Overhead (crowd/pattern) · POV (immersion) · Low-Angle (power/hero) · Macro (water/petal detail) · Dolly Zoom (unease) · Match Cut (link two shots by shape/motion) · Slow Motion (reveal unseen detail) · Dutch Angle (instability/danger) · Framing (frame-in-frame via window/door) · Ultra-Wide (vast/surreal space) · Time Lapse (time passing) · Dolly (smooth rail in/out).
Combos: Low-Angle + backlit silhouette = HERO shot. Macro + translucent + refracting light = beautiful product.

## 6. UGC 5-BEAT + CHECKLIST + SCALING

### 5-beat timestamp structure
0–3s Hook (pattern interrupt) · 3–6s Problem/Observation (relatable) · 6–10s Product Interaction (natural handling) · 10–13s Benefit Payoff (casual, no hype) · 13–15s Casual Close/CTA.

### Pre-generate checklist (UGC)
- [ ] Opens with `UGC creator` / camera identity (iPhone, handheld)?
- [ ] At least 1 imperfection (overexposed / grainy / imperfect skin / natural light)?
- [ ] Dialogue in quotes + `says directly to camera` + <15 words?
- [ ] Timestamps split 0–3 / 4–9 / 10–15?
- [ ] Closes with `no music, no logo, no text on screen`?
- [ ] Aspect 9:16 for Reels/TikTok?
- [ ] Product anchored with @Image1 if the label must be exact?

### Modular scaling (1 product → 15–30 videos)
Build once: 1 character identity pack + 5 hook templates + 10 product-interaction scripts + 3 lighting environments → mix any hook + script + environment = 1 unique video.

### 8-step production pipeline
platform (Higgsfield/kie.ai) → character ref image (identity ONLY, not scene) → product ref (label sharp, logo centered, neutral light, undistorted) → Seedance config (9:16, 15s, hyper-realistic, 1080p, ambient) → master prompt → hook → combine → scale.

## 7. EMOTIONAL-ARC WORKED EXAMPLE (verified on Higgsfield i2v, 15s, 720p)
Brief: Japanese woman, golden-hour rooftop, smile → sad → tears → wipes, in 15s. Result: arc near-exact, identity stable, tears realistic.
Formula: convert each emotion % into a MUSCLE micro-beat per timecode (0-3 / 3-7 / 7-11 / 11-15), not adjectives:
- inner-brow lift + draw together · lower-lid tighten · throat swallow · chin tremble · breath catch · tear spill · nostril flare · slow blink.
i2v handling: no re-describing the face; short positive identity-lock + describe only performance/motion; 4 motion layers separated; tears + glasses catching golden hour = free reflective complexity; final-frame cue = head lowers, hand wipes under glasses, eyes wet, restrained.
3 tuning fixes (if it overacts):
1. "smile 20%" opens too wide → use "faint closed-lip smile, barely there" and drop the word "smile."
2. "very slow push-in" reframes too much → "hold framing, minimal push-in" to keep the frame.
3. Peak nears an open-sob → "lips stay pressed, no open mouth" to contain it.

## 9. REFERENCE TAG SYNTAX (varies by host — CHECK THE UI)
Two conventions exist; use whichever your host UI shows:
- **@-style:** `@Image1` (identity) · `@Image2` (costume/product) · `@Image3` (environment) · `@Image4` (composition) · `@Video1` (motion) · `@Video2` (camera) · `@Audio1` (rhythm).
- **Tag-style:** `[reference_image: file] + [identity_lock]` · `+ [first_frame_lock]` (start on that frame, then animate) · `+ [style_transfer]` · `+ [composition_lock]`. Video: `[reference_video: file] + [camera_copy] / [motion_transfer] / [sequence_extend]`. Audio: `[reference_audio: file] + [beat_sync] / [lip_sync]`.
Discipline: start with one reference type, generate a base clip, then add more next round. On conflict, declare priority in plain text. kie.ai i2v also uses `first_frame_url` + `asset://{assetId}`.
